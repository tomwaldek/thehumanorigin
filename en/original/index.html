The foundation

The human is the original technology.

Everything else is a copy catching up.

Before any system, a nervous system. Before any strategy, a body deciding safe or not. Before any performance, a person who was simply there. Everything since is a copy of a capacity we already are. The machine is only the newest copy. The older ones were drawn up by empires, and they are still running. If you feel the pull to hand your thinking and creating to the machine — and something refuses — this is the case for why, and the ground to stand on while you take it back.

The stance
Don't defend the human. Return to it.

This isn't a war against what's new. The human doesn't need defending — it needs remembering. The capacities we're being sold as replaceable are the originals. Everything else is the imitation, catching up.

The older machine
The machine isn't new. Only the wiring is.

Long before the algorithm, there were systems built to flatten the human into something useful and obedient — colonial, patriarchal, racist orders that decided whose humanity counted, and trained everyone to abandon parts of themselves to survive. AI is the newest layer on a very old machine.

The method never changed. Take from a people what they know, take from a person what they know of themselves, and put in its place whatever serves whoever holds the ledger. Their language, their land, their body, their gods, the way they raise a child — declared primitive, then overwritten by a standard nobody in the room had chosen. Conquest became curriculum. Curriculum became common sense.

And the occupation doesn't end when the flag comes down. It moves inside. It becomes the voice that measures you against that standard and finds you slightly wrong — too much, too emotional, too foreign, too soft, too loud, too slow. Most people meet that voice as their own. It was taught.

The bill was never split evenly. It falls hardest on the people those orders were never built to hold — first, and longest: in the room, in the body, in what they leave at the door to be let in. Anyone who has walked into a room and quietly made themselves smaller knows the mechanism from the inside. For some it happens once. For others it is every room, every day.

What was taken

The knowledge that kept the human whole — the wisdom of indigenous peoples who never split the person from the body, or the body from the land — wasn't lost. It was erased, on purpose, because a whole human is hard to rule.

What replaced it is the thing we now call normal. A self cut into parts, one of them acceptable at work. Thinking without a body. Producing without a season. Knowing without belonging anywhere. That isn't human nature. It's a decision someone drew up, and someone else signed.

Now hand that record to a machine. Centuries of who counted and who didn't — weighted, scored, and handed back with no accent and no hesitation. The same verdict as before, only faster and harder to argue with. That is the continuity the AI conversation keeps walking past.

What's still only ours

They'll tell you the machine creates now. It arranges what already exists, brilliantly. But creation — the thing that comes from a body that has actually lived, lost, wanted — has never left the human. It can't be trained on what it's never felt.

And intelligence that feels: the kind that reads a room before a word is spoken, that tells what's true from what's performed. No model has a nervous system. That reading is ours — and it's the most valuable thing in any room.

The making
The machine gets the output. Never the making.

It skips the part that matters. The sweat. The circling — the fifty wrong turns before the one that lands. The fear of making something, and the fun of it in the same breath. The learning curve that changes you as you climb it. A machine arrives at an answer; a person is transformed by the search for it.

That process — messy, embodied, personal — is the richness. It can't be trained, because it was never information. It's what a body does when it makes something with everything it has: instinct, nerve, feeling, and the willingness to fail in the open. That's yours. The one wealth no system can take, and the one edge no machine will ever close.

You are the original. Use it. Live it.

Taking it back
The last colony is the one inside.

Reclaiming the original means reclaiming from all of it — from the machine, and from the older machine underneath it. Not by studying it. By catching the moment your body edits you into something acceptable, and choosing, once, not to.

With others, or not at all
The last instruction the old order left behind is that you fix yourself privately — alone, before anyone sees. In that instruction, the occupation is still working. A body that was overwritten in relationship does not come back in isolation. Say the true thing in front of one person who doesn’t flinch — that is the smallest unit of the way back, and there is no smaller one.

In a room you lead, the same question turns outward. Whose way of being counts as professional here. Who has to translate themselves before they get heard. Who gets called difficult for saying the true thing. A room changes when the person at the head of the table changes what counts as normal in it.

What we're missing isn't new. It's what was taken.
