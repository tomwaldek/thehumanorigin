The foundation

The human is the original technology.

Everything else is a copy catching up.

Before any system, a nervous system. Before any strategy, a body deciding safe or not. Before any performance, a person who was simply there. Everything since is a copy of a capacity we already are. If you feel the pull to hand your thinking and creating to the machine — and something refuses — this is the case for why, and the ground to stand on while you take it back.

The stance
Don't defend the human. Return to it.

This isn't a war against what's new. The human doesn't need defending — it needs remembering. The capacities we're being sold as replaceable are the originals. Everything else is the imitation, catching up.

What's still only ours

They'll tell you the machine creates now. It arranges what already exists, brilliantly. But creation — the thing that comes from a body that has actually lived, lost, wanted — has never left the human. It can't be trained on what it's never felt.

And intelligence that feels: the kind that reads a room before a word is spoken, that tells what's true from what's performed. No model has a nervous system. That reading is ours — and it's the most valuable thing in any room.

The making
The machine gets the output. Never the making.

It skips the part that matters. The sweat. The circling — the fifty wrong turns before the one that lands. The fear of making something, and the fun of it in the same breath. The learning curve that changes you as you climb it. A machine arrives at an answer; a person is transformed by the search for it.

That process — messy, embodied, personal — is the richness. It can't be trained, because it was never information. It's what a body does when it makes something with everything it has: instinct, nerve, feeling, and the willingness to fail in the open. That's yours. The one wealth no system can take, and the one edge no machine will ever close.

You are the original. Use it. Live it.
The older machine
The machine isn't new. Only the wiring is.

Long before the algorithm, there were systems built to flatten the human into something useful and obedient — colonial, patriarchal, racist orders that decided whose humanity counted, and trained everyone to abandon parts of themselves to survive. AI is the newest layer on a very old machine.

The knowledge that kept the human whole — the wisdom of indigenous peoples who never split the person from the body, or the body from the land — was erased on purpose, because a whole human is hard to rule. Reclaiming the original means reclaiming from all of it. What we're missing isn't new. It's what was taken.
