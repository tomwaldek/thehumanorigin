That decision runs the room, the relationship, the culture — before anyone in it is conscious of it. If you've felt the same pattern shape your leadership, your closest relationship, and your own life, this is the layer underneath all three — and the door to each.

Under pressure, people and organisations fall back on what once kept them safe — performing, controlling, going quiet.

Where this comes from

I was born three months early. The first five months ran inside an incubator — no skin contact on record. In my second year, chemotherapy and surgery for liver cancer. The months a body is meant to learn safety through another's warmth and rhythm, I spent being kept alive by machines.

The steadiness most people are handed by default was never built in me. So I built it from the inside — on sets, in businesses, in rooms I wasn't supposed to be in, and in the rooms where I finally stopped pretending otherwise.

That is where the precision comes from. Not a credential. A nervous system shaped by the absence of another body to settle against in its first weeks, rebuilt on purpose — the reason I recognise, in any room, what others have learned to look past.

Twenty-five years in film. Most of it spent building rooms where people have to be honest on cue — and finding out what it takes. Writing, directing, acting, editing, producing, intimacy coordination. Nobody on a set regulates alone — that isn’t the soft part of the industry, it’s the production schedule. Today I read leadership rooms with the same craft: as founder of The Human Origin — from Vienna, across Europe.

The pattern
A nervous system never finds safety alone.

It borrows it — from another body, and from the space around it. When there's no one to settle against, the body improvises. It performs. It controls. It goes quiet. And it hands what it learned on — to a team, to a child, to a culture that calls the whole arrangement normal.

And safety was never only a private matter. A body reads the room it's standing in — and not every room was built to welcome everyone in it. Some of the oldest arrangements we still live inside — colonial, patriarchal, racial — decided long ago whose natural state counts as welcome. The cost falls hardest on the people those systems were never built to hold. And it costs least the people who decide what counts as normal in the room. If you’re reading this from the head of a table, that’s you — not as an accusation, but as the only position from which any of it changes.

Same mechanism, wherever it runs. A leadership table. A person carrying an inherited idea of who they're allowed to be. A body that never learned it was safe to be touched. Same root. Different rooms.

The other half

Nobody regulates themselves. That is not a weakness — it is the design. A nervous system borrows its safety from another one. That is why isolation makes people harder, not stronger: a body with nothing to settle against starts reading every room as a threat — and then performs to survive it. You cannot optimise your way out of a two-person problem.

Which means the pattern doesn’t only travel through people. It also dissolves through them. One person who stops performing changes what the body next to them expects. That is the whole mechanism of culture — running in the direction nobody is using.

And I am not the counter-example. I am the invoice. What most people are handed before they can speak, I had to manufacture — and I spent thirty years paying the difference. That is how I know what a second body is worth. I priced it.

What was borrowed can be paid off. The clearest sign: the person who used to disorganise you walks in. You stay organised. They didn’t change. You did.

We didn’t lose depth. We stopped building the rooms where it was survivable.

The science

None of this is mine to have invented. It resonates with a century of work — Porges on how the body reads safety, Frankl on meaning as the thing survival replaces, Edmondson on what teams will and won't say out loud, Keltner on what power does to perception. Film sets showed me the patterns before I had the names. The science confirms what the room already knew.
