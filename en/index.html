Where this comes from

I was born three months early. The first five months ran inside an incubator — no skin contact on record. In my second year, chemotherapy and surgery for liver cancer. The months a body is meant to learn safety through another's warmth and rhythm, I spent being kept alive by machines.

The steadiness most people are handed by default was never built in me. So I built it from the inside — on sets, in businesses, in rooms I wasn't supposed to be in, and in the rooms where I finally stopped pretending otherwise.

That is where the precision comes from. Not a credential. A nervous system shaped by the absence of another body to settle against in its first weeks, rebuilt on purpose — the reason I recognise, in any room, what others have learned to look past.

Twenty-five years in film — writing, directing, acting, editing, producing, intimacy coordination. Today I read leadership rooms with the same craft: as founder of The Human Origin — from Vienna, across Europe.

The pattern
A nervous system never finds safety alone.

It borrows it — from another body, and from the space around it. When there's no one to settle against, the body improvises. It performs. It controls. It goes quiet. And it hands what it learned on — to a team, to a child, to a culture that calls the whole arrangement normal.

And safety was never only a private matter. A body reads the room it's standing in — and not every room was built to welcome everyone in it. Some of the oldest arrangements we still live inside — colonial, patriarchal, racial — decided long ago whose natural state counts as welcome. The cost falls hardest on the people those systems were never built to hold.

Same mechanism, wherever it runs. A leadership table. A person carrying an inherited idea of who they're allowed to be. A body that never learned it was safe to be touched. Same root. Different rooms.

The science

None of this is mine to have invented. It resonates with a century of work — Porges on how the body reads safety, Frankl on meaning as the thing survival replaces, Edmondson on what teams will and won't say out loud, Keltner on what power does to perception. Film sets showed me the patterns before I had the names. The science confirms what the room already knew.
